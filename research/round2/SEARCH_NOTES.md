# Bounded-search notes

10 November 2026. Supporting research notes; no triage or experiment results.

The round used sixteen web search/open batches, moving from contribution-level alternatives to direct overlap searches. Topics included refusal versus epistemic abstention, authorization/instruction hierarchy, text-to-tool transfer, transient steering and cache persistence, state rollback/erasure, and fixed-edit quantization. Follow-up primary HTML reads checked the method details of GCAD v2, KVEraser v3, Aborted but Not Forgotten v1 and QERA v1. No new claim uses search absence as evidence.

Three tempting broad questions were not elevated into additional candidates:

- **Does removing safety refusal also remove uncertainty abstention?** Directly adjacent work includes [A Unified Mechanistic Analysis of Knowledge- and Safety-Based Refusals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00760), the [dual-direction ICML 2026 workshop author repository](https://github.com/aaliyan1230/dual-direction-mech-interp), and [Causal evidence that language models use confidence to drive behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01293-x). These were discovery/exclusion checks, not full independent validations of their complete methods. A cross-ablation study alone would need a clearer remaining increment.
- **Does text refusal predict safe tool use or permission enforcement?** [Before Retrieval Begins](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23071v1) already investigates the text/tool-admission relation. [Present but Rescaled](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09156v1) already studies chat-to-agent steering with matched controls and re-encodes transcripts to exclude cache contamination. A further recent title, *Behavior Beyond Text: Evaluating Refusal Transfer Failure in LLM Agents* (arXiv:2610.28982), surfaced but was not independently read; its secondary summaries were not used as evidence for numerical claims.
- **Do steering/cached-state effects persist?** The main report's four closest sources already make generic persistence, contamination, text-matched cache audits and efficient functional repair crowded ground. The proposed main question survives only as a question about separating withdrawn numerical control from retained content memory. It has no priority certification.

An architectural supporting check, [Residual Streams Read, Recurrent States Remember](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32102v1), studies recurrent-state access and steering. Its methods distinguish an edited concept entering output from successfully using it to answer a relation; its limitations warn that cross-model differences do not isolate architecture. It does not justify treating a residual direction as a recurrent-state repair map.

Important version check: **KVEraser v3**, revised 29 September, includes tool-selection and malicious skill-file evaluation, plus a LoRA editor. Reviewing only its June v1 QA evaluation would understate overlap. Its target remains an identified contiguous text span, and it explicitly targets functional rather than exact state equivalence. The report uses v3 coverage.
